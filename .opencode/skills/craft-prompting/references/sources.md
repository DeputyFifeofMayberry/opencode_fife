# Research notes and attribution

Reviewed 2026-10-15. These are instructional sources, not controlled evidence that a framework guarantees better outputs. The skill's examples are original. Its operational rules are a synthesis adapted to this user's preference for direct, concise writing.

## CRAFT definition used in this skill

Use **Context, Role, Action, Format, Tone** consistently. This follows Revolia and the detailed sections and template in Chauhan's article. It is a local convention, not a claim that every CRAFT framework uses these meanings.

## Revolia

[The CRAFT prompt framework](https://www.revolia.pro/blog/craft-prompt-framework), published July 8, 2026.

Uses Context, Role, Action, Format, Tone. Useful ideas include explicit output requirements, task verbs, concrete style guidance, and examples that clarify expected results. This skill retains five sections even for short prompts because the user explicitly requested consistent CRAFT formatting. It does not adopt guarantees about first-attempt success or require the model to reveal its reasoning.

## Crafting AI Prompts

[Crafting AI Prompts](https://craftingaiprompts.org/) and its [IPE framework](https://craftingaiprompts.org/documentation/framework), framework page updated August 7, 2026.

Its prompt framework expands CRAFT as Context, Register, Acting Role, Format, Task. Here, Register maps to Tone, Acting Role to Role, and Task to Action. Its emphasis on interaction, goals, appropriate handling of sensitive information, validation, and continued improvement informs the review loop. Its separate software-engineering CRAFT methodology is outside this skill's scope. This file visibly credits the framework and links its source rather than reproducing its manual.

## Nagesh Chauhan on Medium

[Mastering the CRAFT Method of Prompt Engineering: Guide for Better AI Outputs](https://medium.com/@nkchauhan003/mastering-the-craft-method-of-prompt-engineering-guide-for-better-ai-outputs-b458f43026ba), published November 23, 2025.

The detailed headings and reusable template use Context, Role, Action, Format, Tone. An introductory list instead says Requirements and Tone/Tweak; this skill follows the detailed definition. The article contributes practical technical examples, explicit constraints, and iterative refinement. Its numerical productivity and accuracy claims are anecdotal and are not adopted as guarantees or validation results.

## Additional design choices

Intent preservation, instruction/data separation, conditional tool use, concise evidence instead of private chain-of-thought, explicit unknown values, and honest reporting of testing are reliability choices in this skill. They are not presented as direct quotations or proven effects of the three sources. Reusable prompts need evaluation on their intended model and inputs; a structural skill validator cannot establish output quality.
