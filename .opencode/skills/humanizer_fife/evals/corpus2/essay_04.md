# In Praise of Boredom: The Skill We've Unlearned

We live in an age where every idle second can be filled. Waiting rooms become scrolling sessions, and commutes become podcast marathons. But what if boredom isn't the enemy — what if it's a lost superpower?

At its core, boredom serves three functions: it resets attention, it invites reflection, and it seeds creativity. A reset mind focuses better. Reflection clarifies values. Creative insight often arrives not despite boredom, but because of it.

But what truly makes boredom valuable is what it demands of us. When we resist the urge to fill every gap, our minds wander productively. When we let children be bored, they learn to invent.

It's worth acknowledging the discomfort. The first minutes without a screen feel like withdrawal. However, research on mind-wandering suggests our best ideas surface precisely when we stop chasing stimulation.

The route back to boredom isn't complicated — it's a decision. Put the phone away and let your mind do what it evolved to do: wander, wonder, and connect. Boredom isn't emptiness. It's the beginning of thought.
