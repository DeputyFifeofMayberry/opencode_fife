# Review: The Nimbus Weather Station — Data for People Who Love Data

Weather stations used to be hobbyist gear with hobbyist accuracy. The Nimbus changes that calculus: after six weeks side-by-side with official readings, it has proven itself something genuinely new.

Right out of the box, assembly takes twenty minutes and the app walks you through calibration with meticulous clarity. At its core, the Nimbus's ultrasonic wind sensor and all-weather temperature array delivered readings within 2% of the nearby airport station throughout the test.

But what truly showcases this device is the data pipeline. Moreover, every reading streams to a personal dashboard with API access, meaning your backyard microclimate becomes a queryable dataset — rainfall totals, heating degree days, frost warnings.

It's worth noting the friction points. The $329 price is unapologetically enthusiast-tier. Furthermore, the solar panel wants more sun than winter latitudes provide, and the app's graphs, while deep, took a weekend to learn.

The verdict: the Nimbus is robust, precise, and unapologetically for data lovers. Would I recommend it? For gardeners, homesteaders, and anyone who checks the forecast hourly — absolutely. It turns weather from something that happens to you into something you understand.
