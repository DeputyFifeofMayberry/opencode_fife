# How to Repot a Houseplant Without the Guesswork

Repotting a houseplant can feel risky, but with the right timing and technique, your plant will thank you with new growth. Here's how to do it right.

## Know When It's Time

First and foremost, look for the signs: roots circling the pot or escaping the drainage holes, water rushing straight through, or growth that has stalled during the growing season. Additionally, most plants appreciate repotting every 12 to 18 months.

## Choose the Right Pot and Soil

Next, it's important to select a pot only one to two inches wider than the current one — too large, and wet soil can sour around the roots. Moreover, always use fresh potting mix formulated for your plant type; garden soil is too dense for containers.

## The Repotting Process

Water the plant a day ahead to ease the transition. Gently loosen the root ball, tease apart circling roots, and settle the plant at the same depth it was growing before. Furthermore, fill in with fresh mix, water thoroughly, and skip fertilizer for about a month.

With these steps complete, your plant is positioned to thrive. Happy repotting!
