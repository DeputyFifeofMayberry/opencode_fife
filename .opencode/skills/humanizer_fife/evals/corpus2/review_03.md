# Review: The Breville Barista Express Still Justifies Its Reputation

After two months and roughly two hundred shots, the Breville Barista Express has earned a permanent spot on my counter. It remains, for most people, the best entry point into real espresso.

Right out of the box, the Express makes a statement: conical burr grinder, PID-controlled thermocoil, and a proper 54mm portafilter, all in one machine. At its core, the built-in grinder is what separates this from cheaper "espresso machines" — fresh grinding is the single biggest upgrade you can make.

But what truly wins you over is the workflow. Moreover, the steam wand is genuinely capable, producing microfoam suitable for latte art after some practice. The machine warms up in about a minute, and routine cleaning takes five minutes a week.

It's worth noting the learning curve. Dialing in a grind takes patience, and the included tamper is mediocre — budget for an upgrade. Furthermore, the grinder retention means switching beans wastes a little coffee.

The verdict: at $700, the Barista Express is not cheap, but it delivers café-quality shots without the pro-machine price. Would I recommend it? For anyone serious about espresso, absolutely.
