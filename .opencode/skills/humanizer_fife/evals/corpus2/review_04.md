# Review: The Sony WH-1000XM5 Sets the Bar, Again

Sony's WH-1000XM5 arrives with a familiar challenge: improving a flagship that was already excellent. After a month of commuting, flying, and open-office testing, the answer is yes — mostly.

Right out of the box, the redesign makes a statement. The new shape sheds the folding hinge for a lighter, cleaner frame, and the plush earcups disappear onto your head. At its core, the XM5's noise cancellation is the best in class: airplane rumble vanishes, office chatter drops to a whisper, and the vacuum test is a total rout.

But what truly elevates the experience is the call quality. Moreover, the new four-beam microphone array makes phone calls crisp even on windy streets — a first for this category.

It's worth noting the tradeoffs. The headphones no longer fold flat into a smaller case. Additionally, the touch controls occasionally misread a glasses adjustment, and the sound profile leans warm out of the box, though the equalizer fixes that in minutes.

Ultimately, the WH-1000XM5 is the best all-around noise-cancelling headphone available, at $399 it should be. Would I recommend it? Without hesitation.
