# How to Start a Sourdough Starter From Scratch

Making your own sourdough starter may seem intimidating, but with patience and a kitchen scale, you can have a thriving culture in about a week. Let's break it down.

## Day 1: Begin the Culture

First and foremost, combine equal weights of flour and lukewarm water in a clean jar. A 50-gram start is plenty — stir until no dry flour remains, then cover loosely and leave it at room temperature.

## Days 2–5: Feed and Observe

Discard half of the mixture and feed it fresh flour and water every 24 hours. Additionally, look for signs of life: bubbles, a sour aroma, and a rise in volume. Don't be discouraged by a quiet day or two; starters often stall before they take off.

## Days 6–7: Test for Readiness

Moreover, a starter is ready when it doubles within four to six hours of feeding and smells pleasantly tangy. Perform a float test by dropping a spoonful into water — if it floats, you're ready to bake.

With a healthy starter in hand, you're ready to enjoy the deeply rewarding world of sourdough baking. Happy baking!
