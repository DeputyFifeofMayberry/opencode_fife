# Review: Ride1Up Core-5 — The Budget E-Bike That Doesn't Feel Budget

E-bikes under $1,500 usually feel like compromises on wheels. After two months and 300 miles on the Ride1Up Core-5, I can report something surprising: this one doesn't.

Right out of the box, the Core-5 makes a strong first impression. The 750-watt rear hub motor delivers brisk, quiet assist up to its 28-mph class-3 limit, and the hydraulic disc brakes — rare at this price — inspire genuine confidence. At its core, this is a commuter's bike, and it behaves like one.

But what truly earns my praise is the restraint. Ride1Up skipped the gimmicks: no app-pairing fanfare, no folding frame. Moreover, the torque sensor makes pedaling feel natural rather than motorized.

It's worth noting the tradeoffs. The stock tires are adequate rather than inspiring, and the battery range of 30–40 realistic miles demands a charger on your desk for daily commuters. Furthermore, assembly takes about an hour with basic tools.

The verdict: at $1,095, the Core-5 delivers class-3 performance that used to cost double. Would I recommend it? For car-light commuters, emphatically — it's the value pick in its class.
