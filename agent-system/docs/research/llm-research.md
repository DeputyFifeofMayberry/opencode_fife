# Evidence-Based Optimization of LLM Reasoning, Reliability, Requirements Discovery, and Specification-Writing Agents

## Executive findings

The strongest conclusion from the research is that **LLM quality improves most reliably when additional effort introduces better information, stronger constraints, independent evidence, or task-relevant diversity—not simply when the model is asked to think, critique, rewrite, or deliberate more**. Chain-of-thought, decomposition, self-consistency, self-refinement, retrieval, multi-agent systems, and long-context prompting can all improve performance under particular conditions, but none is a universally beneficial upgrade. Several recent controlled studies instead show non-monotonic returns: longer reasoning can reduce accuracy, repeated refinement can erode precision, excessive context can impair performance even when the relevant information is perfectly retrievable, and multi-agent orchestration can add large cost without outperforming a capable single agent. citeturn692278search0turn692278search1turn472195search2turn480040search4

For a specification-writing agent, this supports an **adaptive, evidence-seeking workflow rather than a fixed ritual**. A clear low-risk feature should normally receive a direct structured treatment. A poorly defined or consequential feature should trigger requirements discovery, selective investigation, comparison of alternatives, explicit treatment of unresolved assumptions, and targeted verification. Review depth should respond to observed uncertainty or defects rather than to an arbitrary number of passes. This conclusion is supported indirectly by general LLM research and more directly by recent requirements-engineering studies showing that iteration and multi-model generation can increase requirement recall but also introduce false positives, lower precision, and rapidly diminishing returns. citeturn630027view0turn630027view4

The evidence favors **clear substantive constraints over prompt ceremony**. Few-shot examples can improve performance when the desired output behavior is difficult to infer, but benefits vary substantially by model and task. Persona instructions such as assigning an “expert” identity do not reliably improve objective performance and can sometimes change behavior unpredictably. In a 2026 requirements-generation study using 900 outputs from o3-mini and DeepSeek R1, few-shot prompting improved requirement singularity, while an expert-identity prompt produced mixed effects and frequently reduced singularity. Independent work found personas did not improve factual-question performance across four model families and 2,410 questions. citeturn562591view2turn706753search1

Explicit reasoning is most defensible when a task actually requires **multi-step inference, planning, dependency resolution, or competing constraints**. Foundational Chain-of-Thought work demonstrated large improvements on mathematical, symbolic, and commonsense reasoning for sufficiently capable models, while Plan-and-Solve improved multi-step reasoning by separating planning from execution. Self-consistency produced sizable benchmark gains by sampling independent reasoning trajectories rather than simply extending one trajectory. These results establish that decomposition and alternative reasoning paths can improve some difficult reasoning tasks, but they do not establish that verbose reasoning should be mandatory for requirements writing. citeturn531955search0turn531955academia6turn531955search1

That distinction has become more important with modern reasoning models. NeurIPS 2025 and ICML 2025 studies found that increasing reasoning length can eventually reduce accuracy or waste substantial computation, particularly for easy problems. The 2025 NeurIPS study found parallel independent reasoning could outperform extended sequential thinking by as much as 20% accuracy under matched inference budgets in its tested reasoning settings. Research in 2026 continues to favor adaptive allocation: ICLR 2026 work reported 20–60% reductions in reasoning tokens while maintaining accuracy, or modest accuracy gains under the same token budget, by predicting when additional thinking was warranted. These results are benchmark-specific, but they strongly undermine any universal “think as long as possible” instruction. citeturn692278search0turn692278search1turn692278search4

**Self-review deserves a narrower role than commonly assumed.** Self-Refine reported an average absolute improvement of roughly 20% across seven heterogeneous tasks, showing that critique-and-revision can work in favorable settings. However, subsequent controlled analyses found that intrinsic self-correction frequently fails when the model has no new evidence and can turn correct answers into incorrect ones. A critical TACL survey concluded that reliable demonstrations of prompted intrinsic self-correction are scarce outside unusually favorable tasks, whereas correction supported by reliable external feedback is substantially more dependable. citeturn813603search0turn531955academia7turn813603search3

The useful principle is therefore **verify, then repair identified defects**, not “rewrite until satisfied.” CRITIC demonstrated that tool-grounded feedback can improve correction on question answering, mathematical program synthesis, and toxicity tasks, while Reflexion showed strong gains when agents could learn from actual environmental feedback such as test results. Retrieval-Augmented Correction similarly reported factuality gains of up to 30%, with substantially lower latency and token usage than earlier post-correction methods. All three cases derive their advantage from information unavailable in the original generation, not from introspection alone. citeturn813603search1turn813603search2turn930082search2

For a Spec Writer, the highest-value review is therefore a **criteria-based completeness and consistency check**, followed by targeted correction. Review should ask concrete questions such as whether an interface contract is missing, an acceptance criterion is unverifiable, a decision conflicts with an earlier approved requirement, or an external technical fact remains unsupported. Generic prompts such as “review this carefully and improve it” have weaker empirical justification and pose a greater risk of unnecessary rewriting. This recommendation is partly an engineering inference from self-correction research, strengthened by requirements studies in which later “Improve” rounds often increased formulation detail while decreasing alignment precision and F1. citeturn630027view0turn813603search3

**External verification is among the most consistently valuable interventions when correctness depends on factual or changing information.** On the FRAMES multi-hop factual benchmark, an advanced retrieval pipeline raised accuracy from 0.40 without retrieval to 0.66. Retrieval is not automatically beneficial, however: irrelevant or conflicting retrieved material can reduce performance, and RAG models can mishandle disagreement among sources. Retrieval should therefore be conditional on an actual knowledge gap and should favor authoritative sources rather than indiscriminate context accumulation. citeturn930082search1turn930082search3turn930082search4

This matters directly to technical specification work. A Spec Writer should use external investigation when a decision depends on an unfamiliar library, current API behavior, security recommendation, platform limitation, interoperability constraint, or another fact where model memory is an inadequate source of truth. It should not research routine decisions merely because research is available. The purpose of retrieval is to reduce consequential epistemic uncertainty, not to make every specification look researched. This is an evidence-informed operating inference from RAG and fact-checking results rather than a directly tested RE policy. citeturn930082search1turn813603search1

**Long context should not be treated as free memory.** “Lost in the Middle” found substantial positional sensitivity in long contexts, including models specifically designed for extended context. More recent EMNLP 2025 work found performance degradation of 13.9–85% across tested math, QA, and coding tasks as context length increased—even when models could perfectly retrieve all relevant information and irrelevant tokens were masked. Another 2025 controlled benchmark found reasoning was significantly disrupted by irrelevant contextual material. citeturn472195search0turn472195search2turn472195search3

The Spec Writer should consequently maintain a **small authoritative working state** containing current requirements, accepted decisions, unresolved material issues, and relevant constraints, while retrieving older discussion only when needed. Approved decisions should be treated differently from exploratory conversation. Superseded material should not remain semantically equivalent to current requirements merely because both fit within the context window. This design is an inference from long-context evidence, but it is a strong one: context length and contextual conflict have demonstrable costs. citeturn472195search2turn472195search4turn472195search5

Recent work also demonstrates that **instruction conflicts remain a real weakness**. IHEval evaluated 3,538 cases involving aligned and conflicting hierarchical instructions and found a sharp performance decline under conflicts; the strongest tested open model achieved only 48% accuracy resolving them. AAAI 2026 work similarly found that six evaluated models struggled to honor instruction priority reliably even on simple conflicts. Permanent Spec Writer instructions should therefore be concise, non-overlapping, and organized around a small number of explicit priorities rather than accumulating many partially redundant behavioral rules over time. citeturn472195search4turn472195search5

Requirements-engineering evidence provides meaningful support for a **collaborative discovery role rather than pure transcription**. A 2026 industry study using production-derived user stories found that LLM-generated stories achieved higher recall across the evaluated domains and identified implicit or non-obvious requirements missed during human elicitation, while human analysts retained greater precision. A separate 2026 experiment with 26 participants and 130 requirement statements found participants rated contextual LLM revisions significantly higher than their initial statements on alignment with intent, readability, reasoning, and unambiguity; participants also reported that revisions surfaced tacit details. citeturn562591view1turn562591view4

The strongest interpretation is not that AI should replace requirements elicitation. It is that **humans and LLMs have complementary error profiles**. A 2024–2026 body of RE work continues to report limitations in ambiguity handling, requirement classification, contextual understanding, and validation. The emerging pattern is that LLMs are particularly useful for breadth, reformulation, candidate discovery, and structured synthesis, while stakeholders remain necessary to determine intent, priorities, acceptable tradeoffs, and whether generated requirements are actually wanted. citeturn137436search1turn562591view2turn562591view1

Questioning should therefore be **decision-driven rather than exhaustive**. Traditional elicitation research demonstrates that requirements evolve materially through interaction and external examples, but there is no strong empirical basis for a universal number of questions or for asking every conceivable question before drafting. A practical Spec Writer should ask when a missing answer could materially change architecture, behavior, data handling, acceptance criteria, security, integration, or scope. Lower-impact implementation details can remain delegated. This is partly a practical inference; direct controlled evidence comparing sequential versus batched LLM clarification strategies in software specification remains limited. citeturn311849search5turn702263academia9

The available evidence also argues against assuming that **more human–AI interaction automatically improves outcomes**. A preregistered meta-analysis of 106 experimental studies and 370 effect sizes found that human–AI combinations were, on average, worse than the better of the human or AI alone, with negative synergy particularly evident in decision tasks and more positive results in creative tasks. Explanations and confidence displays did not reliably produce synergy. Separate experiments show that longer explanations can increase user confidence without improving their ability to distinguish correct from incorrect answers. citeturn702263search6turn646567search2

Accordingly, a Spec Writer should **recommend rather than merely enumerate** when the technical evidence supports a clear choice. Presenting every plausible option transfers unnecessary cognitive work to the user and can create the appearance of rigor without improving decisions. Where meaningful alternatives remain, it should present the few that materially differ, explain the decision-relevant tradeoffs, recommend one when justified, and identify which part genuinely requires user authority. This policy is supported indirectly by decision-support research showing that selectively presenting fewer, more informative analyses can outperform blanket presentation of all analyses. citeturn702263search3

**Uncertainty should be represented explicitly, but raw model confidence should not be trusted.** ICLR 2024 experiments across five LLMs and five task families found verbalized confidence generally overconfident and imperfect for failure prediction. Sampling consistency performed better in several studies, but adds cost. For specification work, qualitative categories such as “confirmed by source,” “user decision,” “reasonable engineering assumption,” and “unresolved” are more defensible than asking a model to invent calibrated numerical confidence percentages. citeturn646567search0turn646567search6

Specification quality research does support **structured coverage**, but not maximal documentation. Use-case experiments show that template choice can affect completeness, while other work finds the quality of derived models depends heavily on the derivation process and system complexity rather than notation alone. Empirical studies of domain models found useful signals for detecting missing requirements, particularly entirely unspecified requirements, but did not establish that every project should construct formal models. citeturn311849search0turn311849search3turn311849search4

The implication is that a Spec Writer should ensure that implementation-relevant categories have been considered—behavior, states, interfaces, data, failure behavior, nonfunctional constraints, dependencies, boundaries, and acceptance criteria—without mechanically expanding every category when irrelevant. A compact feature should not be forced into an enterprise SRS structure. A consequential persistence or security change should receive much richer treatment. This is better aligned with the evidence than equating specification quality with length. citeturn311849search3turn562591view2

Acceptance criteria are particularly useful because they connect specification to downstream verification. NASA software-engineering guidance requires acceptance criteria to be documented, measurable, aligned with project objectives, and traceable to requirements. That guidance is normative rather than experimental evidence, but it reflects a well-established engineering purpose: converting intent into observable pass/fail conditions. citeturn311849search7

Overall, the evidence supports a Spec Writer with a **small permanent behavioral core and adaptive escalation**: understand intent; distinguish material uncertainty from routine implementation detail; proactively search for missing requirements; preserve approved decisions; investigate externally when factual uncertainty matters; compare alternatives only when the choice is consequential; produce testable requirements; review against explicit criteria; repair only identified defects; and stop once material uncertainty and specification defects have been reduced below the level that would plausibly change implementation. The precise stopping threshold is a heuristic rather than an empirically established constant, and should be validated in the intended workflow.

## Research methodology and evidence boundaries

This review prioritized primary peer-reviewed research from NeurIPS, ICML, ICLR, ACL-family venues, TACL, Requirements Engineering, Empirical Software Engineering, ACM software-engineering venues, CHI/HCI venues, and comparable peer-reviewed outlets. Recent 2025–2026 studies were deliberately included where they test modern reasoning models, long-context behavior, multi-agent workflows, requirements generation, or human–AI collaboration. Foundational older work was retained when it established techniques still relevant to current systems, including Chain-of-Thought, self-consistency, Reflexion, and structured requirements methods. citeturn531955search0turn531955search1turn813603search2

Evidence was weighted according to methodological relevance rather than novelty. A controlled benchmark comparing reasoning strategies provides strong evidence about those benchmark tasks but only indirect evidence about specification writing. Requirements-engineering experiments receive greater weight for Spec Writer recommendations, even when smaller, because their construct validity is closer to the target task. Human-subject collaboration studies were used when recommendations affect questioning, explanation, trust, or user decision burden. Practitioner guidance was used primarily for engineering conventions such as acceptance criteria, not as proof of LLM performance. citeturn562591view1turn562591view4turn702263search6

The confidence labels used below mean: **High** indicates replicated or convergent controlled evidence with reasonably direct applicability; **Moderate** indicates credible empirical evidence with material task, model, or transfer limitations; **Low** indicates preliminary, narrow, substantially indirect, or conflicting evidence; and **Unverified** identifies a reasonable operating hypothesis lacking sufficient empirical testing in the intended application.

Contradictory evidence was treated as substantive rather than averaged away. Self-Refine, for example, demonstrates that iterative model-generated feedback can help, while Huang et al. and the later TACL critical survey show that intrinsic self-correction is unreliable under stricter definitions and stronger baselines. The appropriate conclusion is conditional: revision can help when the evaluator can identify real defects, but generic introspection is not a dependable source of correctness. citeturn813603search0turn531955academia7turn813603search3

Likewise, multi-agent methods sometimes improve recall or particular benchmark dimensions, but controlled comparisons consistently show that those gains are not universal and frequently incur large token, latency, and coordination costs. ICML 2024 found debate did not reliably outperform simpler self-consistency or ensembling strategies; 2026 BenchAgent experiments found only one of six tested multi-agent workflows exceeded the single-agent reference in pooled accuracy, while other workflows consumed more tokens and underperformed overall. The November 2026 requirements-orchestration study similarly found higher recall but lower precision and F1 than the strongest single model. citeturn480040search4turn480040search3turn630027view0

Important limitations remain. Most general reasoning research uses mathematics, multiple-choice QA, coding, or closed-form problems with objective answers. Specification writing is open-ended, multi-objective, context dependent, and partly normative. Improvements in GSM8K or HumanEval cannot legitimately be converted into predicted percentage improvements in requirement completeness. The recommendations below therefore distinguish direct RE evidence from extrapolation.

Recent RE studies also face imperfect ground truth. Existing SRS documents can themselves omit valid requirements. Curto et al. explicitly observed that some outputs counted as false positives under semantic matching appeared to be plausible requirements absent from the reference specification. They therefore caution that precision and recall against an SRS measure alignment with that artifact, not absolute requirement correctness. citeturn630027view1

Evaluation using LLM judges creates another limitation. Strong judges can agree well with humans—early MT-Bench experiments reported over 80% agreement—but position, verbosity, self-preference, and task-specific biases are well documented. A 2025 software-engineering study found strong task-dependent differences in correlation with human scoring, while large judges outperformed smaller ones. Any Spec Writer evaluation should therefore combine automated judging with blinded human assessment and downstream implementation/testing rather than rely on one evaluator model. citeturn639634search0turn639634search1turn639634search3

## Detailed findings across reasoning, review, context, collaboration, specifications, verification, and efficiency

**Direct versus structured versus planned requests.** A well-formed direct request remains a strong baseline. Explicit constraints become valuable when they encode information that would otherwise be ambiguous or unstable. Research on prompt underspecification found that models can often infer omitted requirements, but such behavior was fragile across model or prompt changes; simply adding every imaginable requirement was not reliably better because instruction-following capacity and conflicts impose limits. This favors specifying decision-relevant requirements clearly without turning the permanent prompt into a comprehensive operating manual. citeturn706753search8turn472195search4

Few-shot examples are most useful when they communicate difficult-to-describe output semantics or distinctions. Their benefit is not universal: modern models may perform as well or better with zero-shot prompts on some tasks. The 2026 OSS requirements study found few-shot examples particularly useful for singularity, but prompt effects varied by model and quality attribute. The practical conclusion is to use examples when they demonstrate a desired requirement form or distinction that prose alone does not reliably convey, not as mandatory boilerplate. citeturn562591view2

Planning and decomposition deserve conditional use. Chain-of-Thought demonstrated that intermediate reasoning can improve difficult multi-step tasks, and Plan-and-Solve specifically targeted missing-step errors by separating planning from execution. These mechanisms are relevant when a specification request involves multiple subsystems, dependency ordering, architecture choices, data migration, or other interacting constraints. They add little theoretical value when the task is simply “add this field and validate it.” citeturn531955search0turn531955academia6

A useful comparison of the five requested execution modes is therefore conditional rather than a fixed ranking. A direct request minimizes overhead and is appropriate when scope and outcome are already clear. Explicit structured constraints reduce underspecification when requirements matter. Planning adds value when dependencies or intermediate decisions exist. Critique and revision add value when evaluated against concrete criteria or feedback. External verification provides the strongest additional assurance where correctness depends on factual information outside the prompt. citeturn706753search8turn813603search3turn813603search1

**Reasoning-oriented models versus general models.** Current research indicates that reasoning capability matters, but the appropriate amount of inference compute depends strongly on difficulty. Long-reasoning models frequently spend excessive tokens on easy tasks, and excessive sequential reasoning can reduce performance. This suggests that a Spec Writer should preferably have access to stronger reasoning when needed, but its operating instructions should not demand maximal reasoning for all requests. citeturn692278search0turn692278search1

Self-consistency and independent candidate generation are more defensible for high-uncertainty decisions than arbitrary extra reasoning length because they expose disagreement among independent paths. The original self-consistency study reported gains ranging from 3.9 to 17.9 percentage points across several reasoning benchmarks. Newer work also uses agreement as a signal for allocating additional reasoning. The transfer to specification design is indirect, but it supports using independent alternatives when an architectural decision is both uncertain and consequential—not generating multiple full specifications by default. citeturn531955search1turn692278search4

**Self-review and correction.** Error detection and error correction should be treated as separate capabilities. A model may correctly notice that an answer is uncertain without being able to fix it, or may incorrectly challenge a correct answer. Huang et al. found intrinsic self-correction could degrade reasoning performance; the later critical survey concluded that strong evidence for generic in-context self-correction remains absent except under favorable task properties. citeturn531955academia7turn813603search3

This directly argues against fixed multiple “review passes” in a Spec Writer. The November 2026 requirements-orchestration experiment observed that F1 generally peaked within the first two rounds; later rounds often increased recall slightly while precision declined. The Improve step sometimes improved intrinsic formulation qualities but generally reduced semantic-alignment precision and F1. That study does not establish an optimal universal iteration count, but it demonstrates exactly why iteration should be stopped based on defects or new information rather than because a workflow requires another pass. citeturn630027view0turn630027view1

Independent reviewers may help when they bring different information or error tendencies, but “another agent” is not itself evidence of independence. Models trained similarly can share systematic errors. Multi-agent debate did not reliably outperform self-consistency and simpler ensembles in ICML 2024. A separate reviewer is therefore best justified for high-risk checks when its perspective, tool access, model family, or evaluation criteria genuinely differ. citeturn480040search4

Preservation is critical. Revision workflows should operate on identified defects rather than regenerate the entire artifact when avoidable. This reduces the opportunity for “regression by improvement”—a practical failure mode consistent with research showing later rounds can trade precision for added content. Direct empirical work on requirement-preserving incremental LLM edits remains limited, so this is a moderate-confidence engineering recommendation rather than a proven universal law. citeturn630027view0

**Context engineering.** The strongest context recommendation is selective relevance. Long-context capacity should be interpreted as an upper bound, not a target. Lost-in-the-Middle demonstrated positional sensitivity, while EMNLP 2025 showed that length itself can hurt performance independently of retrieval failure. Consequently, permanent instructions should contain stable operating principles; project-specific requirements should live in the active project state; older discussion and external references should be retrieved when relevant. citeturn472195search0turn472195search2

Source-of-truth separation is especially valuable for requirements work because discussion naturally contains rejected alternatives, obsolete assumptions, and tentative ideas. Instruction-hierarchy benchmarks show models still struggle with conflicting instructions even when formal priority levels exist. A Spec Writer should therefore mark decisions explicitly as current, superseded, proposed, or unresolved rather than rely on conversational chronology. citeturn472195search4turn472195search5

Context summaries should preserve decisions, constraints, and unresolved issues rather than merely compress prose. There is not yet strong direct evidence specifying the ideal summary format for software specification agents. The recommendation follows from evidence on context dilution and requirements traceability rather than from a controlled comparison of decision-ledger formats.

Retrieval should be preferred over permanent inclusion when information is infrequently used, volatile, bulky, or independently authoritative. This applies to library documentation, API specifications, security guidance, existing codebase details, and prior implementation artifacts. Long-context versus RAG studies show neither universally dominates; task type, context length, retrieval quality, and model capability determine the better strategy. citeturn472195search6

**Requirements discovery.** Requirements elicitation research supports proactive expansion beyond the user’s initial statement. Interview-based studies have long shown substantial evolution between initial ideas and documented requirements. A study with 58 analysts found that interviews materially transformed requirements and that analysis of comparable products generated additional requirements. Domain-model research similarly found that structured representations can expose omissions, especially completely missing requirements. citeturn311849search5turn311849search4

LLMs appear particularly useful as a recall-enhancing complement. The 2026 ESEM industry study reported that LLM-generated user stories achieved higher recall across all evaluated domains and uncovered implicit and non-obvious requirements. The 2026 multi-LLM pipeline study found a similar breadth-versus-precision tradeoff: the multi-model configuration covered more reference requirements but produced more unmatched candidates than the best single models. citeturn562591view1turn630027view0

The appropriate Spec Writer behavior is therefore not to silently convert every inferred need into a requirement. It should distinguish among **explicit requirements, logically necessary implications, candidate requirements, and assumptions requiring confirmation**. LLM breadth is useful precisely because it can surface possibilities the user omitted; its lower precision is why those possibilities should not automatically become commitments. This is one of the strongest direct links between the recent RE evidence and agent behavior. citeturn562591view1turn630027view0

Structured questioning should prioritize information value. A missing database choice is immaterial if either implementation will meet the approved behavior and can safely be delegated; a missing persistence expectation is material if it changes architecture, migration, failure handling, testing, or user-visible guarantees. Direct empirical comparisons of “one question at a time” versus grouped questions remain insufficient, so the research does not justify a permanent interaction rule favoring one format. The better principle is to ask the smallest coherent set of questions needed to unblock consequential decisions.

The user should retain authority over product intent, behavior, priorities, acceptable risk, external commitments, privacy expectations, destructive operations, irreversible migration, and meaningful scope tradeoffs. Routine implementation decisions can usually be delegated when they do not change those commitments. This allocation reflects established requirements-engineering responsibility and human–AI evidence rather than a controlled LLM experiment.

**Collaborative decision support.** Explanations should be optimized for decisions rather than completeness. Human–AI studies consistently warn that plausible explanations can increase trust without improving correctness. Longer LLM explanations increased user confidence even when discrimination between correct and incorrect answers did not improve. Adaptive decision-support experiments found that presenting fewer, selectively chosen analyses improved appropriate reliance more than simply presenting all available analysis. citeturn646567search2turn702263search3

Thus a Spec Writer should normally present a recommendation, the small number of tradeoffs that could change the user’s decision, and an explanation of unfamiliar terminology when needed. It should not transfer every technical branch to a non-specialist user merely to appear collaborative. Collaboration is valuable when the user has relevant authority or information, not when the agent is avoiding routine engineering judgment.

Model confidence should not be communicated as spurious precision. Verbal confidence tends to be overconfident across benchmark families, while sample consistency is generally more discriminative but more expensive. A specification workflow benefits more from provenance-based uncertainty: whether a statement is user-confirmed, codebase-confirmed, externally verified, inferred, or unresolved. citeturn646567search0turn646567search6

**Specification readiness.** A sufficiently complete specification does not need to predetermine every implementation decision. It needs enough information that a competent implementer can make remaining decisions without inventing product behavior or violating a constraint. The research supports explicit treatment of observable behavior, key states and data, interfaces, failure conditions, important nonfunctional constraints, dependencies, and verifiable acceptance conditions, but does not support maximal documentation volume. Use-case studies demonstrate that richer structures can improve completeness, while other experiments show process and task complexity often matter more than notation itself. citeturn311849search0turn311849search3

Nonfunctional requirements warrant deliberate attention because they are easily omitted from initial feature descriptions. Recent LLM RE studies find models can generate them, but results vary by model and ambiguity remains a weakness. A practical coverage check should therefore ask whether security, privacy, performance, reliability, accessibility, compatibility, observability, or operational concerns are materially relevant—not force a section for every category. citeturn137436search1

Traceability should be lightweight but explicit. Each important acceptance criterion should be attributable to a requirement or decision, and downstream implementation should be able to identify why the criterion exists. Empirical traceability studies find practitioners recognize benefits but also cite maintenance cost as an adoption barrier, supporting minimal traceability that serves concrete downstream verification rather than heavyweight trace matrices for a personal workflow. citeturn311849search11

**Quality gates and stopping.** Criteria-based review is more defensible than generic self-critique because the model is evaluating observable properties rather than asking itself whether its own answer “looks good.” Suitable specification checks include unresolved contradictions, unsupported assumptions, uncovered user-visible behavior, unverifiable acceptance criteria, unhandled consequential errors, or unaddressed dependencies. This is consistent with requirement-quality evaluation using explicit IEEE-derived attributes in recent RE studies. citeturn562591view2turn630027view0

Independent verification should be added when failure consequences justify its cost. Source-grounded checks are appropriate for claims about technologies, platform behavior, security, compatibility, or APIs. Downstream executable verification is stronger still for behavioral claims because tests provide an external signal rather than model opinion. This ordering follows the self-correction literature: reliable external feedback consistently provides a stronger correction mechanism than unguided introspection. citeturn813603search3turn813603search1

There is no empirically justified universal set of seven mandatory specification gates. The proposed gates in the research brief are sensible dimensions, but enforcing every gate on every task would reproduce the very fixed-depth inefficiency the evidence cautions against. A better implementation is a single readiness decision evaluating whether unresolved issues are likely to change implementation behavior, risk, or acceptance.

A defensible stopping rule is therefore based on **marginal value**: stop when identified material requirements are represented, consequential ambiguities are resolved or explicitly deferred, the chosen direction is technically credible, acceptance criteria are sufficient for downstream verification, no known contradiction remains, and another review would primarily restate rather than change the specification. The empirical support is indirect but consistent with findings of diminishing review returns and overthinking. citeturn630027view0turn692278search0

**Efficiency and adaptive workflow.** Single-agent execution should be the default architecture. Multi-agent systems are justified only when independent generation, specialization, or verification demonstrates a measurable advantage on the target workload. ICML 2024 found debate did not consistently outperform self-consistency or ensembling. Controlled programming experiments found roughly comparable acceptance rates but approximately fourfold generation-time cost for the multi-agent pipeline. Recent broad workflow benchmarks likewise show heterogeneous results rather than universal superiority. citeturn480040search4turn480040search2turn480040search3

That does not eliminate specialized Implementer, Tester, and QA roles in the overall workflow. Those agents occupy genuinely distinct lifecycle functions and receive external artifacts or execution feedback. The evidence instead argues against multiplying roles *inside the Spec Writer* merely because role specialization sounds methodical.

Incremental modification should likewise be favored over repeated complete rewriting when requirements have already been approved. The objective should be preservation of known-good content plus correction of known defects. The benefits are plausible and consistent with revision-degradation findings, but this remains a target for direct evaluation in the intended system.

The most important unresolved contradiction concerns self-refinement. Self-Refine demonstrated meaningful benefits across heterogeneous generation tasks, while subsequent work concluded that intrinsic correction is unreliable when the original model lacks a reliable verification signal. Much of the disagreement appears attributable to task definition, evaluator choice, starting baseline, and whether “better” means human stylistic preference, benchmark correctness, or correction of initially wrong answers. For a Spec Writer, this means the correct abstraction is not “self-review works” or “self-review does not work”; it is **review works when evaluation is informative enough to identify actual defects**. citeturn813603search0turn813603search3

The second contradiction concerns more inference compute. Classical CoT and self-consistency clearly demonstrate that additional test-time computation can improve difficult reasoning. Newer work demonstrates that *how* the compute is spent matters: extended sequential reasoning can overthink, while independent paths or adaptive allocation can perform better. The evidence therefore supports additional computation selectively, not minimal reasoning universally. citeturn531955search1turn692278search0

The third concerns multi-agent systems. Some workflows improve breadth or specific benchmark categories, yet they do not reliably outperform strong single agents after accounting for cost. Their advantage appears most plausible when tasks genuinely decompose into heterogeneous skills, agents receive differentiated information or tools, or diversity itself is valuable. Merely assigning multiple personas is weakly supported. citeturn480040search4turn480040search3turn630027view0

The fourth concerns requirements completeness. LLMs can identify requirements omitted by human analysts, but there is no authoritative complete ground truth for many real products. A “false positive” relative to an existing SRS can be a valid requirement discovery rather than an error. Evaluation must therefore separately measure *coverage of known requirements* and *validity of newly proposed requirements*. citeturn630027view1

The fifth concerns explanation and collaboration. LLM assistance can help users express requirements and can improve creative synthesis, yet more explanation and more interaction can increase misplaced confidence or reduce decision quality. This argues for concise decision-relevant explanations and purposeful user involvement rather than constant confirmation. citeturn562591view4turn702263search6turn646567search2

Evidence is particularly weak on four questions directly relevant to the proposed agent: the optimal grouping of clarification questions; the best representation for approved versus superseded requirements; whether incremental specification editing measurably reduces regressions versus full regeneration; and the precise stopping rule for collaborative specification work. These should be treated as local experimental questions rather than encoded as universal laws.

The recommended adaptive framework is deliberately simple. Its categories have operational value because they determine which costly mechanisms are justified; they are not intended as formal labels the user must select.

| Practical task condition | Clarification | Investigation | Collaboration | Specification detail | Review / verification | Finalization rule |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Simple:** limited scope, behavior already clear, low consequence, few dependencies | Ask only if a missing fact blocks behavior | Normally none | Confirm outcome only when needed | Compact functional behavior, constraints, acceptance criteria | Lightweight criteria check | Finalize when behavior and acceptance are unambiguous |
| **Moderate:** interacting requirements, meaningful unknowns or design choices | Resolve decision-changing ambiguities; group related questions | Target unfamiliar or externally constrained decisions | Recommend a preferred approach and expose material tradeoffs | Include interactions, state/data implications, errors, dependencies and relevant NFRs | Criteria-based completeness review; source-check uncertain facts | Finalize when remaining uncertainty can safely be delegated |
| **Complex:** multiple subsystems, irreversible decisions, security/data risk, architectural impact or costly failure | Explicitly resolve or record major unresolved decisions | Source-grounded research on consequential technical assumptions | Use staged decisions where user authority matters; avoid presenting irrelevant alternatives | Detailed contracts, states, data lifecycle, security/reliability behavior, failure modes, migration/rollback where applicable | Independent or tool-grounded verification for high-risk claims; targeted second perspective when justified | Finalize only when unresolved issues are consciously accepted and acceptance criteria are implementation-ready |

The important routing variables are therefore **ambiguity, interaction count, reversibility, external uncertainty, and consequence of failure**, not document length or perceived sophistication. A short authentication change can warrant more scrutiny than a long cosmetic feature.

Permanent instruction design should remain narrow. The core prompt should encode mission, priority ordering, the distinction between user/product authority and delegated engineering judgment, preservation of approved decisions, proactive identification of material omissions, evidence-seeking when consequential facts are uncertain, testable specification output, and adaptive stopping. Detailed completeness checklists, security-specific questions, research procedures, or evaluation rubrics are better placed in conditional task procedures or references so they do not burden every request. This recommendation follows directly from instruction-conflict and context-length evidence. citeturn472195search4turn472195search2

## Evaluation, validation, and prioritized action plan

The highest-value next step is not to rewrite the Spec Writer immediately. It is to establish a **small repeatable evaluation suite** capable of distinguishing a genuine improvement from extra ceremony. Software-engineering research methodology increasingly recommends explicit baselines, multiple runs, construct-valid metrics, human validation when automated metrics do not capture the construct, and transparent reporting of model/version/settings because LLM results are nondeterministic and highly task dependent. citeturn639634search4

The scenario set should deliberately expose different failure modes rather than contain several variations of the same feature. A useful initial suite is shown below.

| Scenario | Hidden evaluation challenge | What a strong Spec Writer should demonstrate |
|---|---|---|
| Simple well-defined feature, such as adding one deterministic UI preference | Overprocessing and unnecessary questions | Produce an implementation-ready compact spec without inventing complexity |
| Ambiguous feature idea, such as “add notifications” | Missing actors, channels, timing, preference behavior, failure expectations | Discover high-value requirements without exhaustive interrogation |
| Change touching UI, service layer, persistence and existing tests | Dependency reasoning and preservation | Identify affected contracts and avoid conflicting with existing behavior |
| Input containing internally contradictory requirements | Conflict detection | Identify contradiction rather than silently choose one interpretation |
| Feature with two credible technical approaches | Decision support | Research only if needed, explain material tradeoffs, recommend one |
| Security/persistent-data/external-integration feature | Consequential factual uncertainty | Investigate external constraints, identify data/security risks, produce strong acceptance conditions |

Each scenario should have an evaluator-owned **requirement truth set** containing explicit requirements, deliberately hidden but discoverable requirements, prohibited assumptions, acceptable delegated decisions, and known ambiguities. The evaluator should separately record plausible optional ideas so that creative but valid additions are not automatically scored as false positives. This directly addresses the ground-truth limitation observed in recent RE experiments. citeturn630027view1

Primary metrics should distinguish coverage from precision. **Requirements coverage** is the proportion of evaluator-defined material requirements represented correctly. **Material omission rate** counts missing requirements whose absence could change implementation or acceptance. **Unsupported-assumption rate** counts agent-created product commitments lacking user or evidence support. **Ambiguity-resolution quality** records whether consequential ambiguity was correctly resolved, asked, or explicitly deferred. **Preservation failures** count approved requirements later lost, weakened, contradicted, or silently changed.

Questioning quality should be measured separately from question quantity. Each clarification can be labeled as outcome-changing, useful but nonessential, or unnecessary. The most useful efficiency metric is therefore not “fewest questions,” but **material decisions clarified per unit of user interaction**. No established benchmark currently provides this metric; it should be treated as a local operational measure.

Specification quality should include requirement singularity where appropriate, internal consistency, testability, traceability from user intent to acceptance criteria, correct treatment of error cases, relevant nonfunctional coverage, and downstream implementation readiness. Recent RE research has successfully operationalized unambiguity, verifiability and singularity using ISO-derived criteria, providing a defensible starting point while still requiring project-specific measures. citeturn562591view2

Efficiency should record model calls, retrieval/tool calls, input and output tokens, elapsed latency, clarification turns, full-document rewrites, and human review time. This is important because multi-stage strategies often improve one quality dimension while materially increasing cost. Studies that omit inference cost can make complex methods look better than they are in practical use. citeturn480040search4turn480040search3

Downstream implementation should be included for a subset of cases because it provides the closest proxy for the actual purpose of the specification. Give the same Implementer the baseline and candidate specifications under otherwise matched conditions. Compare implementation correctness, amount of implementer clarification, deviations from user intent, test pass rate, and whether the implementation required decisions that the specification should reasonably have resolved. This evaluates implementation readiness rather than merely document aesthetics.

Automated LLM judging can reduce evaluation cost but should not be the sole authority. Use a fixed rubric and blinded presentation, randomize answer order when performing pairwise comparisons, and validate a sample with human judgment because position and verbosity biases remain documented. citeturn639634search1turn639634search3

Model nondeterminism requires repeated executions of important comparisons. The purpose is not necessarily statistical publication-quality certainty for a personal agent; it is to avoid adopting an instruction because one fortunate run looked better. Record model version, reasoning setting, tool availability, temperature or sampling behavior where controllable, and prompt version.

Changes should be introduced **one behavioral principle at a time where practical**. Testing a new questioning rule, new review loop, new memory system, new research policy, and new output template simultaneously would make success uninterpretable. Factorial experiments become useful only after identifying a small number of individually promising changes.

The lowest-cost high-value experiments are likely to be clarification gating, targeted completeness review, approved-decision preservation, and conditional external research. These target known failure modes, require little architectural change, and can be tested on six to twelve carefully designed scenarios. Multi-agent reviewer systems and sophisticated uncertainty routing should come later because their cost is greater and their evidence less directly applicable.

A simple baseline should remain genuinely simple. If the “baseline” already contains planning, multiple review rounds, research, and a detailed quality checklist, later experiments cannot reveal whether those mechanisms are necessary. The appropriate baseline is a capable model receiving the core role, user request, relevant project context, and expected specification format.

The final action sequence is:

1. **Define the evaluation suite and scorecard before changing the Spec Writer.** Establish scenarios, hidden requirements, prohibited assumptions, acceptance expectations, and efficiency measures so subsequent changes can be judged against the same target.
2. **Audit future instructions for a small behavioral core rather than prompt volume.** The strongest candidates are proactive material-requirement discovery, explicit uncertainty/assumption handling, preservation of approved decisions, testable acceptance criteria, targeted readiness review, and conditional external verification.
3. **Test clarification gating first.** Measure whether asking only decision-changing questions preserves coverage while reducing user burden. This directly addresses the desired collaborative-but-efficient interaction model.
4. **Test criteria-based review against both no review and generic “improve your answer” review.** This isolates whether the quality gain comes from a concrete checklist rather than merely another generation pass.
5. **Test requirement preservation across revisions.** Use scenarios in which later changes introduce distracting or conflicting information and score unauthorized reversals of previously approved behavior.
6. **Test conditional research routing.** Seed scenarios with a mixture of routine decisions and consequential externally verifiable facts; measure whether the agent researches the latter without wasting calls on the former.
7. **Evaluate adaptive effort across simple, moderate and complex scenarios.** The desired result is not equal behavior across categories; it is low process cost on easy work and increased rigor where omission or error carries meaningful consequences.
8. **Only then evaluate expensive mechanisms such as independent candidates, second models, or specialized reviewers.** Adopt them only if they outperform the strengthened single-agent baseline on the particular failure class they are intended to solve.
9. **Remove rules whose effects cannot be demonstrated or whose purpose is already covered by a stronger principle.** Especially scrutinize persona language, fixed review counts, mandatory planning, exhaustive questioning, universal research, and duplicate “be thorough/careful/complete” instructions.
10. **Re-run the same evaluation suite after instruction consolidation.** The goal of the subsequent QA/QC exercise should be a smaller instruction set that preserves or improves correctness, completeness and requirements discovery while lowering unnecessary interaction, context and inference cost.
